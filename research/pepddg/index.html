Peptide AI / 2026

PepDDG.

Peptide–protein binding ΔΔG prediction via information channel decomposition.

NeurIPS 2026

Official conference listing ↗

01 / The problem

Rank single-point peptide mutations by their impact on protein binding.

02 / The core idea

Equal-weight rank fusion of energetic perturbation, geometric environment and evolutionary compatibility; calibrated residual variant reported separately.

How the idea flows

03 / What the public evidence shows

Author-authorized V4 reports pooled Spearman 0.619 for zero-shot PepDDG and 0.691 for PepDDG-Cal on 332 peptide-chain mutations across 33 targets, using crystal structures. Official conference index verifies the title; acceptance is author-confirmed.

Conference listing verification: PepDDG ↗

04 / Where the claim stops

Ranking only, not absolute kcal/mol error. Single-point scope; stabilizing mutations and inaccurate binding poses are important failure regimes. V4 is not claimed as camera-ready; individual author role not inferred.

An editorial scope note, not an unverified quotation of author limitations.

AUTHOR-AUTHORIZED V4 / ORIGINAL FIGURE 1

Three kinds of evidence. One ranking problem.

PepDDG transforms energetic perturbation, geometric environment and evolutionary compatibility into rank space, then combines them with equal-weight Borda rank-sum. Larger scores indicate more destabilizing mutations. Its output is an ordering, not calibrated kcal/mol predictions.

01 / Energetic perturbation

OpenMM with Amber ff14SB / OBC2; paired wild-type / mutant restarts construct binding-energy and interface-interaction proxies.

02 / Geometric environment

Wild-type interchain heavy-atom contacts (8 Å) and Cα neighbors (10 Å). Position-only: different substitutions at the same site receive the same geometric signal.

03 / Evolutionary compatibility

Pretrained ProteinMPNN complex / isolated-chain likelihood differences and mutation likelihood ratios. No task-specific fine-tuning; the pretrained model itself is learned.

Deployment ranks candidates within a target; the pooled benchmark ranks the full cohort. Training-free means no task-specific training or learned fusion weights, not that the underlying foundation model was never pretrained.

DATA / CRYSTAL-STRUCTURE BENCHMARK

332 mutations. 33 targets.

PepDDG-Bench is curated from SKEMPI v2: single-point peptide-chain mutations with one partner of 5–80 residues, excluding ultra-short peptides, saturation-mutagenesis-dominated targets and preparation failures. Main-benchmark inputs are the associated PDB crystal structures. Experimental ΔΔG > 0 denotes binding destabilization.

V4 §3, §6.1 and Table 1 (pages 3, 7). Metric: pooled Spearman ρ; 95% CIs use target-stratified bootstrap. Values below are manuscript-reported, not independently reproduced by this website work.

ABLATION / WHAT THE CHANNELS CONTRIBUTE

Complementarity carries information.

Cross-channel ablation

Single-channel ρ ranges from 0.469–0.504; two-channel combinations from 0.568–0.574; all three reach 0.619. Pairwise correlations are 0.33, 0.40 and 0.41. Lower correlation does not establish statistical independence.

V4 Figure 2 · pp. 7–8 · N=332

Adding a signal does not always help

Energetic interface proxy alone reaches 0.477, full binding proxy 0.316, and their rank-sum 0.473. An optional alanine fourth channel changes 0.633 to 0.637 on an N=303 subset; this is not directly comparable with 0.619 on N=332.

V4 Appendix Table 9 · p. 23; Figure 2

The theoretical analysis depends on stated rank-covariance conditions; arbitrary channel fusion is not guaranteed to improve performance.

BOUNDED DIAGNOSTICS / STRUCTURE INPUTS

Read strong results with their boundaries.

Short and cyclic peptides

Short peptides (<20 residues): ρ=0.813, CI [0.684, 0.909], 47 mutations / 5 targets. Cyclic peptides: ρ=0.872, CI [0.753, 0.926], 35 mutations / 4 targets.

Retained retrospective diagnostic cohorts, not prospective discovery. With four cyclic targets, exact sign-flip testing has minimum p=0.0625; no p<0.05 claim.

V4 Table 3 · p. 9; Appendix C.9 · p. 23

Predicted structures are a separate diagnostic

On 109 matched mutations, Boltz-2 predicted-input ρ=0.621 and crystal-input ρ=0.592. This differs from the 332-mutation main cohort and does not establish that predicted structures generally outperform crystals.

Uses the highest-ipTM wild-type sample without predictor fine-tuning. Large pose errors and mutation-induced binding-mode changes are not explicitly modeled.

V4 §6.6 · pp. 8–9
